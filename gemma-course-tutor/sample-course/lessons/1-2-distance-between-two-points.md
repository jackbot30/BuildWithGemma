# 1.2 Distance Between Two Points

## Introduction

Once points are placed on the coordinate plane, a common task is to measure the
straight-line distance between two of them. This lesson is original material written
for this sample course.

## The distance formula

Given two points $P(x_1, y_1)$ and $Q(x_2, y_2)$, the distance between them is:

$$d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$$

This comes from the Pythagorean theorem: the horizontal gap $(x_2 - x_1)$ and the
vertical gap $(y_2 - y_1)$ are the two legs of a right triangle, and the distance $d$
is its hypotenuse.

## Worked example

Find the distance between $P(1, 2)$ and $Q(4, 6)$.

1. Horizontal gap: $x_2 - x_1 = 4 - 1 = 3$.
2. Vertical gap: $y_2 - y_1 = 6 - 2 = 4$.
3. Apply the formula: $d = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16} = \sqrt{25} = 5$.

So the distance is $5$.

## Key idea

Subtract the coordinates to get the two gaps, square them, add, and take the square
root. The order of the two points does not matter because the differences are
squared.
