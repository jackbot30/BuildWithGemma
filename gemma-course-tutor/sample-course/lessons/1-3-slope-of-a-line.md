# 1.3 Slope of a Line

## Introduction

The **slope** of a line measures how steep it is and in which direction it tilts.
This lesson is original material written for this sample course.

## The slope formula

Given two points $A(x_1, y_1)$ and $B(x_2, y_2)$ on a line, the slope $m$ is the
ratio of the change in $y$ to the change in $x$ — often remembered as **rise over
run**:

$$m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$

- A **positive** slope rises from left to right.
- A **negative** slope falls from left to right.
- A **zero** slope is a horizontal line.
- A **vertical** line has an undefined slope, because $x_2 - x_1 = 0$ and you cannot
  divide by zero.

## Worked example

Find the slope of the line through $A(1, 2)$ and $B(3, 8)$.

1. Change in $y$ (rise): $y_2 - y_1 = 8 - 2 = 6$.
2. Change in $x$ (run): $x_2 - x_1 = 3 - 1 = 2$.
3. Slope: $m = \frac{6}{2} = 3$.

So the slope is $3$.

## Key idea

Slope is rise over run: subtract the $y$-values, subtract the $x$-values in the same
order, and divide. Keep the point order consistent in the numerator and denominator.
